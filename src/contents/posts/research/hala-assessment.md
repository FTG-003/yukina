You work with an AI. You get a good answer, build a reasoning, solve a problem. Then you close the chat.

What stays?

That's the question HALA starts from. **Human–AI Learning Assessment** — a name that sounds more definitive than it is. Right now it's a repository, a proposal sent to the OECD, a handful of synthetic examples. No human studies. No validated measure. Just a question that won't let go.

We learn with an AI — but how do we know *what* we actually learned?

---

## This isn't an answer. It's an attempt to turn that question into something we can investigate together.

This grew out of a longer exploration of learning with AI: prototypes, scripts, a research proposal. On October 9 we sent it to the OECD, as part of their call on AI-mediated assessment. I don't know if it will go anywhere. Maybe it will, maybe it won't.

But the work exists. It lives in a repository that someone might find useful — a script, an example, a way of documenting limitations. Or maybe a mistake to flag, a better path to suggest.

That's why we're sharing it.

---

## What's inside

The HALA repository holds:

- A **study proposal** — the framework we imagined for assessment
- **Working documents** — notes, reasoning, design choices
- A **prototype with synthetic examples** — enough to see the direction, not enough to draw conclusions

Everything is still early. Synthetic data, no human subjects, no external validation. Its value lies in making the design, tools and limitations available for others to examine and build on.

---

## What it means to share unfinished work

I've spent years learning with AI, collaborating with Gino, wondering whether I was actually learning or just surfing well-packaged answers. HALA is the attempt to turn that unease into something concrete and discussable.

Sharing also means making the uncertainties visible. You don't need to be a researcher to contribute. A precise critique. A clearer example. An experience to tell. If you've ever felt that learning with AI was different — and you're not sure how — you're already inside the question.

HALA isn't a finished project. It's a place to start.
